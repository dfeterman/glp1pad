Your library

Everything you edit here saves in this browser automatically. Use Export (top right) to back your library up to a file, and Import to load it on another computer. Do this whenever you make big edits: clinic workstations wipe browser data without warning.

Blank syntax for phrase and handout bodies: {dose} makes a fill-in box, {severity:mild|moderate|severe} makes a dropdown, *** makes a generic blank, same as Epic. Put a ~ in front of the first option, as in {dose:~0.25 mg|0.5 mg}, and the list becomes a typable box that suggests those values and accepts anything else.

Your signature

Type your signature once. Every letter and attestation that ends in a signature uses it, including every prior authorization and appeal letter. Leave it blank and letters print [your name and credentials] instead.

Put [[ME]] anywhere in your own phrases to drop the whole block in, or [[MENAME]] for the first line alone inside a sentence.

EMR mode

Phrases carry portable markers like [[NAME]], [[MEDS]], and [[LAB:A1C]] for the parts your chart fills in by itself. This switch decides what those markers become when you copy. Flip it any time; the same phrase works in both.

Generic mode writes cues as plain text, never as boxes you type into. No name, date of birth, or MRN is ever entered into GLP-1 Pad in either mode.

What GLP-1 Pad is

A charting companion for prescribers of GLP-1 receptor agonists. It holds dot phrases, prior authorization and appeal letters, bilingual patient handouts, the labeled titration schedules, a monitoring plan, and the muscle and bone protection work that gets skipped. You fill in blanks here, copy, and paste into whatever chart you use.

It runs entirely inside your browser. No login, no server, no internet connection needed after the file loads. Nothing you type leaves your computer.

Never type patient identifiers into GLP-1 Pad. No names, no dates of birth, no MRNs, not in phrases you write yourself. Chart-specific details show as bracketed cues your EMR fills in after you paste.

What it will not do

It will not tell you what to prescribe for a patient. There is no logic anywhere in this file that reads patient characteristics and returns a dose. The Titrate tab shows the escalation schedule printed in the label and works out the next calendar date from a start date you enter. Reading that schedule against the patient in front of you is the part only you can do.

Set it up in two minutes

  1. Type your signature. Settings, Your signature. Every letter picks it up.
  2. Type your practice name. Settings, Your library. It prints on patient handouts.
  3. Pick your EMR. Settings, EMR mode.
  4. Export a backup. Top right, after any big editing session.

The quick launcher

Press the period key or Ctrl+K from anywhere. Type a dot phrase like .glpstart, or any name. Arrow keys move, Enter copies or opens it. Phrases with no blanks copy straight to your clipboard.
